EFFECT OF THE SETTINGS

The bias you choose matters more than search time

Language

Michie: 0.625 → 0.875 after removing ¬ and ∀. A richer language finds more spurious concepts that fit 23 trains.

Noise tolerance

Noisy data: 0.58 → 0.90. With 10 % noise allowed, CELOE returns the true rule; without it, it overfits.

Runtime

Easy targets: 1 s is enough. Michie: 0.625 at 1–5 s, 0.875 at 20 s (155k candidates).

Search algorithm

OCEL returns longer concepts (length 9–19 vs 5) and no answer unless one is 100 % correct on training.

What did each setting change? First, language. On Michie's trains, the full ALC language scores 0.625; removing negation and universal restrictions jumps to 0.875. With only 23 training trains, a bigger hypothesis space contains more short, wrong concepts that fit perfectly. The same happened on Michalski fold 8: CELOE picked "at least three non-jagged wagons". Second, noise: with noise tolerance zero, CELOE overfits the flipped labels; allowing ten percent gives the exact true rule. Third, runtime mostly matters on the hard problem. Finally, OCEL lacks CELOE's preference for short concepts.

LIMIT 1 · EXPRESSIVITY

Michie’s rule needs a variable that ALC does not have

LEARNED (0.875, 21 of 24 folds)

∃hasCar.(DoubleWallCar ⊔ PeakedRoofCar ⊔ (ClosedCar ⊓ RectangleCar))

Peaked roof and double wall are the same conditions human entrants used in Michie’s 1994 competition.

OFFICIAL RULE

a wagon with a peaked roof, or two adjacent short wagons with the same load shape X

“The same X” ties two loads together. One ∃ cannot say that: it needs a 6-way disjunction over load shapes, length over 60.

Concept induction is exact inside its language and blind outside it. A forest has no hard wall, but gives no rule.

Michie's 24 trains show the first limit. The best learned concept says: some wagon has a double wall, a peaked roof, or is a closed rectangle. That gets 0.875 and is very stable, and the peaked roof is half of the official rule. But the other half says two adjacent short wagons carry the same load shape. That "same" is a variable shared between two loads, which description logic without nominals or role chains cannot express with one existential. You could only write it as a disjunction over all six load shapes, a concept of length sixty or more, far beyond what the search reaches. So inside its language the learner is exact; outside it, it is blind.
